# Research brief: locksmith consumer guidance, Arizona licensing & recording law, ID, car keys, home locks

> Desk research, 2026-09-18. Not legal advice. FTC locksmith page (consumer.ftc.gov/articles/0089) now redirects — used the archived 2013 copy + FTC 2008 release + state pages. Reddit unreachable — Section 6 built from locksmith FAQ pages and agency checklists. roc.az.gov blocked fetches — ROC statutes/rules read on azleg.gov and law.cornell.edu.

## 1. What consumers are told to ask/check

**FTC (archived):** car lockout → consider roadside assistance first · be wary of a generic "locksmith services" greeting instead of a company name · get "an estimate for all work and replacement parts … before work begins" · ask about extra fees (mileage, minimum service-call fee, middle-of-the-night charges) · on arrival ask for ID and a business card; invoice, card and vehicle should show the same company name · "Expect the locksmith to ask you for identification" · ask if insured (Wisconsin: write down the insurer) · if the on-arrival price doesn't match the phone estimate, don't allow the work; "Never sign a blank form" · be wary if told up front the lock must be drilled · ask for an itemized invoice.

**BBB (2020):** generic greeting; "$15" quotes that grow on site; push to drill and replace with "extra-secure" lock. Advice: ask about service-call, mileage, parts; written estimate; marked vehicle and ID; itemized invoice with business name/address.

**ALOA:** company you called should match who arrives; uniform/name tag/"non-removable logo or signage"; red flags: straight to drilling, excuses then higher price, cash-only, out-of-state routing, no local presence, heavy ad spend.

**AZ AG:** no locksmith-specific guidance; general contractor advice (Nov 2025): verify ROC, written estimates, avoid cash-only, pay by card.

**Script implications:** company name in greeting · full price incl. trip + night fee before dispatch · tech name + ID requirement · cards accepted + invoice · non-destructive first, drilling explained and priced before · marked vehicle/uniform only if true (owner confirmed: magnet logos + uniform) · honest line for "where's your shop?".

## 2. Arizona licensing

- **No state locksmith license.** ALOA (Jan 2025): 13 licensing states — AL, CA, CT, IL, LA, MD, NV, NJ, NC, OK, OR, TX, VA. Arizona not on it. NASTF 2025 list doesn't include AZ.
- **ROC** licenses "contractors" (ARS 32-1101: construct, alter, repair … any building or structure). C-7/R-7 Carpentry scope lists "Hardware" → door-hardware installation is ROC work unless exempt. Lockouts/rekeys/keys not obviously construction.
- **Handyman exemption ARS 32-1121(A)(14):** "aggregate contract price, including labor, materials and all other items … is less than $1,000"; "casual or minor nature"; not if a local building permit is required; not if part of a larger operation or split to evade; **not for a person who advertises without disclosing unlicensed status with the words "not a licensed contractor" in the advertisement.**
- HB2120 (2025, raise to $3,500) died 06/27/2025; SB1715 (2023, $2,000) not law. Re-check each session.
- **Compliance:** site already says "Not a licensed contractor…" (index.html, llms.txt). Rule (c) = "any form of advertising" → owner should ensure it also appears on GBP, FB/IG, ads, vehicle magnets.
- **Cities:** no locksmith license in Phoenix/Mesa/Chandler/Gilbert/Tempe/Scottsdale. General business licenses: Phoenix none · **Mesa required since 2022 for Mesa-based businesses incl. home-based, $25/yr** · Chandler/Gilbert for fixed locations there · Tempe for TPT holders doing business there · Scottsdale "all business/individuals conducting business within the city" — verify for mobile. A city business license ≠ trade license; don't call it "licensed".

**Honest phone answers:**
- "Are you licensed?" → "Good question. Arizona doesn't have a state locksmith license. We're TrustKey Locksmith, a registered Arizona company, and we carry liability insurance. For installs we're not a licensed contractor, so those jobs stay under $1,000 with no permits — if your job is bigger, I'll tell you up front."
- "What's your ROC number?" → "We don't have one — we're not a licensed contractor, so we keep hardware installs under the state's $1,000 limit."
- Never: "licensed", "licensed & bonded", "state-certified", "ROC-licensed", "ALOA member".

## 3. Call recording — Arizona is one-party consent

- ARS 13-3005(A)(1): felony to intercept a communication "to which he is not a party … without the consent of either a sender or receiver".
- ARS 13-3012(9): exempts interception "with the consent of a party to the communication".
- Out-of-state callers: all-party states incl. CA, CT, FL, IL, MD, MA, MT, NH, PA, WA. *Kearney v. Salomon Smith Barney* (Cal. 2006) applied CA law to out-of-state recorder; "A business that adequately advises all parties … at the outset … would not violate the provision."
- **Recommended:** automatic announcement on every inbound call ("This call may be recorded for quality and training"). OpenPhone (now Quo) has a "Call recording notification" setting. On outbound callbacks, say it live.

## 4. Proof of ownership / ID

- No AZ statute on locksmith ID — industry norms.
- **Cars (NASTF Positive ID policy, 2008 copy):** inform the caller up front · valid state driver's license · proof of entitlement: registration, insurance card/policy, title, or certificate of ownership, name/address matching · documents locked inside → may open the car to check; if not verified, "the vehicle shall be shut and locked. Do not produce a replacement key…"
- **Homes:** photo ID with the service address; if different — lease, utility bill, mortgage/tax statement, mail, or landlord/property manager confirmation; ID inside → open, show immediately; can't verify → relock and/or police non-emergency.
- **Decline:** landlord locking out a current tenant (ARS 33-1367 tenant remedies); another adult resident objects.
- **Commercial:** ID + proof of authority (lease/license naming the person, or letter from owner/manager).
- Script line: "For your protection, the tech will need a photo ID and something in your name for the car or home — like the registration or insurance card, or a lease or utility bill. If it's locked inside, that's fine — you'll show it right after we open up."

## 5. Scam patterns (context only)

Lead-gen call centers with generic greeting + subcontractors · lowball phone quote then "special tools"/"high-security" upcharge · unnecessary drill-and-replace · fake local addresses (Google/UCSD 2017: locksmiths among the most abused Maps categories, ~11% of locksmith results fraudulent) · unmarked vehicles, cash-only, blank forms. **Answer calmly with true facts only; never criticize competitors.**

## 6. Most common phone questions

How much (total incl. trip/night) · any other fees · how soon (honest window, no guarantee) · will you damage it · do I need ID · licensed/insured/bonded · are you local / where's your shop · cards · all keys lost · program my online fob · rekey vs replace / one key for all · cheaper than the dealer (only our own price — no comparisons) · warranty (owner-confirmed only: 30 days).

## 7. Car keys

**Why refuse customer-bought fobs:** FCC ID / part-number mismatch ("cannot be programmed regardless of who attempts it" — 1-800-Unlocks) · wrong frequency (315 vs 433 MHz), wrong chip/encryption, empty shells without transponder (Mr. Locksmith) · used fobs locked to the first car, many one-time programmable (Mercedes dealer: "hard coded to the unique VIN… cannot be reprogrammed") · clones + liability: tech time and equipment used whether or not it works.

**Plain English:** transponder (chip talks to immobilizer; blade turns but engine won't start without it) · remote-head (blade + buttons in one) · flip/switchblade · fob (buttons only) · smart/proximity (push-button start; hidden emergency blade) · high-security laser-cut/sidewinder (cut milled into the flat side; special machine) · all keys lost (code or lock decode, then program; costlier, longer; some need modules or dealer).

**European:** ALOA — dealer sometimes the only option, "most prevalent in European cars". Mercedes FBS3 (≈ up to 2014) usually doable; **FBS4 (≈2015+) generally dealer-ordered by VIN**. VW/Audi (≈2017+) security gateway online unlock; AKL may need multiple module reads. Newer BMW AKL often module removal for bench work. Script: "For European cars we check by VIN before we promise anything."

**Collect on the phone:** year/make/model · push-to-start or turn-key · number of working keys · VIN (dash at windshield, driver door jamb, registration/insurance) · car location/access · license + registration/title/insurance matching.

## 8. Home locks

Rekey = "adjusts your existing locks so they work with a different key" (Schlage); recommended after move-in and lost/stolen keys. Pins lifted to the shear line. Deadbolt (not spring-loaded), knob/lever (spring latch), mortise (most secure common type, commercial), tubular (least secure, interior). **Grades — say:** "Grade 1 is heavy-duty commercial, Grade 2 is medium-duty, Grade 3 is basic residential" (don't quote cycle counts). KW1 (Kwikset) vs SC1 (Schlage) not interchangeable; some Kwikset SmartKey locks come in SC1 → "one key for everything" depends on brands. Many smart locks keep a rekeyable key cylinder.

## Owner to-do surfaced by this research
1. Insurance carrier/limits; bonded or not (answer "bonded?" honestly).
2. Mesa general business license ($25/yr, required since 2022 for Mesa-based businesses) — verify it's in place; check Tempe/Scottsdale.
3. "Not a licensed contractor" wording on EVERY form of advertising (GBP, FB/IG, ads, vehicle magnets) — ARS 32-1121(A)(14)(c).
4. Turn on OpenPhone/Quo call-recording announcement.
5. Written policies: customer-supplied fobs (decided: no), refusing jobs when ID can't be verified.
